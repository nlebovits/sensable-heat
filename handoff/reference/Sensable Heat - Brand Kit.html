001 — Identity

A public reading of urban heat.

Sensable Heat is a global, high-resolution land-surface-temperature map for municipal decision-makers. The brand is plain, instrumented, and quietly serious — built to sit on the desks of the people who plan cities, not the ones who pitch them.

The viewfinder is the brand.

Four corner brackets frame the word sens — naming the satellite-sensing lineage and the deliberate misspelling in one move. The wordmark always sets in IBM Plex Mono Bold, lowercase, with the brackets locked at 2.25 pt and 4 px padding around the targeted glyphs.

04  ·  Color · Neutrals

A near-black and a paper-white. Nothing in between is a brand color.

The interface is dominantly one of two surfaces — a deep blue–black or a warm paper. Hairline lines, mute greys, and text greys sit between them. The heat ramp is the only chromatic system; no secondary brand color competes with it.

05  ·  Color · Heat Ramp

Single-hue red. Stepped by luminance, safe across CVD.

A nine-step sequential ramp built in OKLCH so each step is perceptually equidistant. Hue rotates gently from cool red (25°) to warm red–orange (40°) as the ramp warms, but the discriminability comes from luminance — which keeps it readable for protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia, and full monochromatic vision.

06  ·  Imagery

Bitmap dither. Halftone, never glossy.

All hero and incidental imagery — satellite scenes, globes, supporting illustrations — passes through a 1-bit Atkinson or Floyd–Steinberg dither at ~3 px per dot. The treatment ages well, prints well, and signals “data” without trying to look futuristic. Pin / interest dots may use the heat ramp at L6–L7 sparingly.

07  ·  Voice

Plain. Measured. Slightly literary.

The product speaks like a careful scientist talking to a careful planner. It names the work, gives the number, cites the source, and stops. It avoids alarm, avoids salesmanship, and avoids the word solution.

Tone — yes

  • doState the measurement. “Land surface temperature, P95, annual composite, 30 m.”
  • doCite the source data inline. “Built on Landsat Collection 2 via Source Cooperative.”
  • doUse the active voice for the planner’s action. “Pick a ward. Read the heat.”
  • doLet silence carry weight. Short sentences. White space.

Tone — no

  • noCatastrophic framing. No “deadly,” “unprecedented,” or alarm-styled headlines.
  • noStartup verbs. No “empower,” “unlock,” “leverage,” “democratize.”
  • noAI / agent framing. The tool reads heat, it doesn’t “understand” cities.
  • noEmoji, exclamation marks, or marketing exclamations.
